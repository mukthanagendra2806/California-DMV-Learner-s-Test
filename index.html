The knowledge test, in plain terms

Before you can get a California driver’s license, you have to pass a knowledge test and a behind-the-wheel drive test. Both are based on the California Driver’s Handbook.

The knowledge test is multiple choice. You take it when you apply for your instruction permit, along with a vision test. You can’t use testing aids such as the handbook or your phone. pp. 1–2, 5

How to prepare

  1. Read the official handbookThe tests are based on it. Start with Section 7, Laws and Rules of the Road.
  2. Practice one topic at a timeWork through a topic until you can explain why each answer is right.
  3. Take full mock tests46 random questions with a pass mark of 38. Aim to pass several in a row.

If you fail: you get three attempts to pass the knowledge test before you must reapply.

If you’re under 18, wait seven days to retake a failed knowledge test, not counting the day you failed. A failed drive test means a 14-day wait. pp. 5–6

What the handbook stresses

  • Right-of-way, again and again

    Pedestrians always have the right-of-way. Give up your own right-of-way whenever it helps prevent a collision. p. 39

  • The “never” lists

    Memorize where you may never make a U-turn, park, or pass. Test questions love these. pp. 20, 24, 28–29

  • Shape before words

    An octagon means stop, a triangle means yield, five sides means a school, a circle means a railroad crossing, a diamond is a warning, and a white rectangle is a rule. pp. 35, 38

  • Slowing down for weather

    On a wet road, drop 5–10 mph. On packed snow, drive at half speed. On ice, go no faster than 5 mph. p. 61

  • Your first year

    Two at-fault collisions or convictions mean 30 days of driving only with a licensed adult 25 or older. Three mean a six-month suspension. p. 55

Road signs

All 30 signs below appear in the handbook. Learn the shape and color first, then the words.

Regulatory

Red and white or black and white. These are rules you must obey.

  • Stop

    Come to a full stop.

    Stop at the limit line, or before the crosswalk or intersection. Check all directions before going.

    p. 35

  • Yield

    Let others go first.

    Slow down and be ready to stop for any vehicle, bicyclist or pedestrian.

    p. 35

  • Do Not Enter

    Traffic on this road or ramp comes toward you.

    Don’t enter the road or ramp where it’s posted.

    p. 38

  • Wrong Way

    You’ve entered a road against traffic.

    When it’s safe, back out or turn around. At night, red reflectors in your headlights mean the same thing.

    p. 38

  • No U-Turn

    A red circle with a line through it shows what you can’t do.

    Don’t make a U-turn here.

    pp. 20, 38

  • No Left Turn

    Left turns are not allowed here.

    Go straight or turn right instead.

    pp. 36, 38

  • One Way

    A white or black rectangle shows a rule you must obey.

    Never make a U-turn on a one-way street. You may pass on the right on one.

    pp. 20, 25, 36

  • Do Not Pass

    No passing on this stretch.

    Stay in your lane. Passing near hills, curves and intersections is dangerous anyway.

    pp. 24, 36

  • Left Turn Yield on Green

    A green light doesn’t give you a protected left turn.

    Yield to pedestrians and to oncoming vehicles close enough to be dangerous.

    pp. 36, 39

  • Do Not Block Intersection

    Blocking an intersection is against the law.

    Don’t enter unless you have room to get all the way across before the light turns red.

    pp. 36, 39

Warning

Yellow diamonds, the five-sided school sign and the round railroad sign all warn of something ahead.

  • Slippery When Wet

    The road gets slick when wet.

    Slow down 5–10 mph in rain, and take extra care when rain first starts.

    pp. 37, 61

  • Merging Traffic

    Traffic joins your road from the side.

    Make space for vehicles entering, even if you have the right-of-way.

    pp. 37, 58

  • Lane Ends

    Your lane is about to end.

    Look for a sign telling you to exit or merge, and merge safely.

    pp. 14, 37

  • Traffic Signal Ahead

    A traffic light is coming up.

    Be ready to stop. On yellow, stop if you can do so safely.

    pp. 33, 37

  • Pedestrian Crossing

    People may be crossing ahead.

    Slow down or stop to let pedestrians finish crossing, with or without a crosswalk.

    pp. 37, 41

  • Stop Ahead

    A stop sign is ahead, possibly hidden.

    Slow down and get ready to stop at the limit line.

    pp. 35, 37

  • Curve

    The road curves in the direction shown.

    Slow down before the curve, not in it, and avoid fast turns.

    pp. 37, 60

  • Two Way Traffic

    Oncoming traffic shares this road.

    Stay to the right. Only pass when you can see well ahead and it’s safe.

    pp. 13, 37

  • School

    Five sides means you’re near a school.

    Drive slowly and stop for children in the crosswalk. The limit is 25 mph within 500 ft when children are present.

    pp. 38, 50

  • Railroad Crossing Ahead

    A round yellow sign means a railroad crossing is coming.

    Look, listen, slow down and prepare to stop. Let any trains pass first.

    p. 38

  • Slow-Moving Vehicle

    This orange and red triangle marks vehicles that usually go 25 mph or less.

    Slow down early and pass only when it’s safe.

    pp. 36, 45

Construction

Orange signs mark work zones, where fines can be doubled.

  • Thru Traffic Merge Left

    A lane is closed ahead.

    Merge as soon as it’s safe without crossing the cones or drums.

    pp. 36, 49

  • Road Closed Ahead

    The road ahead is closed.

    Slow down and follow the detour signs, cones and flaggers.

    pp. 36, 49

  • Road Machinery Ahead

    Slow-moving equipment is working on the road.

    Prepare to slow down or stop for highway equipment.

    pp. 36, 49

  • Flagger Ahead

    A worker is directing traffic.

    Obey the flagger’s instructions, and expect to stop.

    pp. 36, 49

  • Shoulder Work Ahead

    Crews are working beside the road.

    Slow down, leave extra space, and watch for bicycles if the shoulder is closed.

    pp. 36, 49

Guide

Green, brown and blue signs point the way to places and services.

  • Airport

    A green guide sign showing the way to an airport.

    No action is required. Use it to plan your lane early.

    p. 36

  • Recreation area

    A brown guide sign showing the way to a recreation site, here cross-country skiing.

    No action is required. It points to a destination.

    p. 36

  • Rest Area

    A blue sign pointing to a service: a rest area one mile ahead.

    If you’re tired, plan to stop. Fatigue slows your reactions.

    pp. 9, 36

  • Railroad emergency sign

    A blue and white sign at a crossing with a phone number and the crossing ID.

    If you stall on the tracks with no train coming, get out and call this number, then 911.

    pp. 38, 70

Handbook summary

The most-tested sections in a few lines each. Page numbers match the 2025 handbook.

  • Permit and license steps pp. 2–6

    • Under 18: be at least 15½, finish driver education, and get a parent or guardian to sign.
    • For the license: be 16, hold the permit 6 months, finish driver education and training, and log 50 practice hours (10 at night) with a licensed driver 25 or older.
    • You get three tries at the knowledge test. Minors wait 7 days after a failure, or 14 days after a failed drive test.

  • Provisional license rules pp. 4, 55, 71

    • First 12 months: no driving 11 p.m.–5 a.m., and no passengers under 20 without a licensed adult 25 or older.
    • Exceptions need a signed note: medical, school, work, or driving a family member.
    • No cell phone use at all while driving, except to call for emergency help.

  • Signals, horn and headlights pp. 10–12

    • Signal 100 ft before a turn, and 5 seconds before a freeway lane change.
    • Turn headlights on from 30 minutes after sunset to 30 minutes before sunrise, and whenever your wipers are on.
    • Dim high beams within 500 ft of oncoming traffic and 300 ft of a car you’re following.

  • Lanes, turns and passing pp. 13–25

    • Don’t pass over double solid yellow lines, and never change lanes over double solid white lines. A broken yellow line next to your lane means you may pass when safe.
    • Center left turn lane: 200 ft at most. A U-turn in a residential district needs 200 ft clear both ways.
    • Don’t pass within 100 ft of an intersection, bridge, tunnel or railroad crossing.

  • Parking pp. 26–29

    • Curbs: white is a passenger stop, green is time-limited, yellow is loading, red is no stopping, blue is disabled parking.
    • On a hill, turn your wheels toward the curb when facing downhill, away from it when facing uphill, and to the right if there’s no curb.
    • Keep 15 ft from a hydrant and 20 ft from a crosswalk.

  • Signals and signs pp. 33–38

    • Treat a flashing red like a stop sign. A flashing yellow means caution, and a red arrow means no turning at all.
    • A dark traffic light means a four-way stop.
    • Yield to pedestrians even when DON’T WALK is flashing.

  • Right-of-way pp. 39–42

    • The first vehicle to arrive goes first. If two arrive together, yield to the one on your right.
    • At a T intersection, the through road has the right-of-way. On a steep mountain road, the vehicle facing uphill does.
    • In a roundabout, yield on entry and travel counterclockwise.

  • Sharing the road pp. 42–52

    • If you can’t see a truck’s mirrors, its driver can’t see you. Pass large vehicles on the left.
    • Leave 3 seconds behind motorcycles and 3 ft when passing bicyclists.
    • When a school bus flashes red, stop from both directions, unless it’s across a divided or multilane highway.

  • Safe driving and conditions pp. 57–64

    • Scan at least 10 seconds ahead, and keep a 3-second following distance.
    • In a skid, ease off the gas, don’t brake, and steer in the direction of the skid.
    • In fog, use low beams. If you can’t see past 100 ft, go no faster than 30 mph.

  • Seat belts, child seats and speed pp. 65–67

    • Everyone buckles up. You can be ticketed for any passenger under 16 who isn’t.
    • Children under 8, or shorter than 4 ft 9 in, ride in a child restraint in the back seat.
    • Under the Basic Speed Law, never drive faster than is safe. The limit is 65 mph on most highways, 55 mph on two-lane undivided roads.

  • Emergencies pp. 68–70

    • Tire blowout: hold the wheel with both hands and ease off the gas gradually. Don’t brake hard.
    • Broken down on the freeway: pull onto the right shoulder, call 511, and stay buckled in.
    • Stalled on the tracks with a train coming: get out and run diagonally toward the train’s direction.

  • Alcohol and drugs pp. 72–75

    • BAC limits: 0.08% at 21 and over, 0.04% for commercial or for-hire drivers, 0.01% under 21.
    • Refusing a chemical test means a one-year suspension. A DUI stays on your record 10 years.
    • Open containers go in the trunk, never the glove box.

  • Insurance and collisions pp. 76–78

    • Minimum coverage is $30,000 for one person’s injury or death, $60,000 for more than one person, and $15,000 for property.
    • Report to law enforcement within 24 hours if anyone is hurt.
    • Report to DMV within 10 days if anyone is hurt or damage exceeds $1,000.
